2. Which LLM to Use for Benchmarking?

You must use meta-llama/llama-3.1-8b-instruct.

GraphRAG's indexing phase (where the model reads your dataset and extracts relationships, nodes, and edges) is incredibly token-heavy, particularly on output tokens.

Referencing the file "image_e27161.png" verbatim, meta-llama/llama-3.1-8b-instruct provides the lowest overall cost footprint for this type of workload on the platform:

    Input / 1M: ₹2.00

    Output / 1M: ₹5.01

For comparison, referencing the file "image_e2744b.png" verbatim, even OpenAI's smallest model (gpt-4o-mini) costs ₹15.02 per 1M input tokens and ₹60.08 per 1M output tokens. Given the massive amount of JSON output required to construct a knowledge graph, gpt-4o-mini would immediately blow past your 20 Rs limit.

Why this works for your budget:
With your 20 Rs maximum budget, using meta-llama/llama-3.1-8b-instruct allows you to process up to ~10 million input tokens or generate nearly ~4 million output tokens. This gives you plenty of runway to run a highly optimized "micro-benchmark" (e.g., 30-50 documents) to prove the multi-hop superiority of GraphRAG over standard RAG and get the necessary latency/cost metrics for your LinkedIn post.

## 3. Which Databases to Use?

You have two architectural paths for your repository, and it depends on how complex you want the setup to be:

**Path A: The Unified Engine (Recommended for your benchmark)**

* **Database:** **ArcadeDB**
* **Why:** In 2026, managing two separate stores (one for vectors, one for graphs) is considered operational overkill for new projects. ArcadeDB is fully open-source (Apache 2.0) and supports both native vector search and graph traversals in a single engine. This makes your open-source repo much easier for others to clone and run.

**Path B: The Enterprise Standard (The "Dual-Store" approach)**

* **Graph Database:** **Neo4j** or **TigerGraph**. Neo4j is excellent for developer ergonomics, while TigerGraph is preferred for massive production-scale GraphRAG.
* **Vector Database:** **Chroma** or **Qdrant**.
* **Why:** This is the canonical pattern. If you want your LinkedIn post to reflect what most enterprises currently have deployed (a messy dual-store sync), use Neo4j + Qdrant.
